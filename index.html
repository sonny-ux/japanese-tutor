Sentence Structure

Japanese is SOV — Subject → Object → Verb. Particles are the glue that show what each word is doing.

The Big Picture: Subject → Object → Verb
ぼく boku subject
は wa topic marker
ラーメン rāmen object
を wo object marker
食べる taberu verb (eat)
"I eat ramen."
Key insight: In casual speech, particles and even subjects get dropped all the time. Just ラーメン食べる？ (rāmen taberu?) = "Wanna eat ramen?" is totally normal.
The Core Particles
は
wa
Topic marker — "as for X" / "speaking of X." Signals what you're talking about.
ぼくは学生だ
boku wa gakusei da
"I'm a student."
が
ga
Subject marker — specifically identifies who/what does the action. More emphatic than は.
だれが来た？
dare ga kita?
"Who came?"
を
wo (o)
Direct object marker — marks what receives the action of the verb.
映画を見る
eiga wo miru
"to watch a movie"
に
ni
Direction / time / location (existence). "to / at / in / on" for destinations and when things exist.
東京に行く
tōkyō ni iku
"go to Tokyo"
3時に会う
sanji ni au
"meet at 3 o'clock"
で
de
Location of action / means / method. "at (doing something) / by / with / using"
カフェで勉強する
kafe de benkyō suru
"study at a café"
電車で行く
densha de iku
"go by train"
と
to
"And" (linking nouns) / "with" (together with someone).
友達と遊ぶ
tomodachi to asobu
"hang out with friends"
コーヒーとケーキ
kōhī to kēki
"coffee and cake"
も
mo
"Also / too / either." Replaces は or が to mean "also."
ぼくも行く
boku mo iku
"I'm going too."
か
ka
Question marker — turns any sentence into a question. In casual speech often just rising intonation instead.
行くか？
iku ka?
"Are you going?"
の
no
Possessive connector. "X's Y" = X の Y. Also nominalizes verbs (turns verb → noun-like).
ぼくの友達
boku no tomodachi
"my friend"
食べるのが好き
taberu no ga suki
"I like eating"
から / まで
kara / made
"From / since" and "until / up to." Often paired together.
9時から5時まで
kuji kara goji made
"from 9am to 5pm"
に vs で — The Trickiest Pair
に
Use に for existence / destination — "where something IS" or "where you're GOING TO"
学校にいる — gakkō ni iru
"(I) am at school" (existing there)
図書館に行く — toshokan ni iku
"go to the library"
で
Use で for action location / means — "where you're DOING something" or "using what"
学校で勉強する — gakkō de benkyō suru
"study at school" (doing an action there)
バスで来る — basu de kuru
"come by bus"
Common Sentence Patterns
X は Y だ / です — "X is Y"
これはおいしい — kore wa oishii
"This is delicious." (casual)
ここは静かじゃない — koko wa shizuka ja nai
"This place isn't quiet." (casual negative)
V-たい — "I want to V"
ラーメン食べたい — rāmen tabetai
"I wanna eat ramen."
V-て / V-てる — progressive / connected action
音楽聞いてる — ongaku kiiteru
"I'm listening to music." (て + いる → てる in casual)
食べてから寝る — tabete kara neru
"I'll sleep after eating." (て-form connects clauses)
〜んだ / 〜んだけど — explaining / softening
ちょっと忙しいんだ — chotto isogashii n da
"The thing is, I'm a bit busy." (んだ gives reason/explanation nuance)
んだ / んだけど is extremely common in casual speech. It softens statements, implies you're providing context, and makes things sound less blunt. If you can use one Japanese phrase a lot, it's this one.

Conjugation Guide

The complete system. Japanese verbs change based on their group — learn the group, learn the pattern.

The three groups: Godan (U-verbs) — the big group, end in う く す つ ぬ ぶ む る (non-ichidan) | Ichidan (Ru-verbs) — end in -iru or -eru, just drop る | Irregular — only する and くる
How to Identify the Verb Stem
Godan (U-verbs)
Change the final う-sound vowel to get different forms
書く → stem: 書
kaku → ka-
飲む → stem: 飲
nomu → nom-
Ichidan (Ru-verbs)
Drop the る — what remains is the stem
食べる → stem: 食べ
taberu → tabe-
見る → stem: 見
miru → mi-
Irregular — just memorize
Only 2 fully irregular verbs
する suru → "to do"
来る kuru → "to come"

Godan Te-Form Cheat Sheet — The Sound Change Rules
く → いて / ぐ → いで
書く → 書いて kaku → kaite
泳ぐ → 泳いで oyogu → oyoide
⚠️ Exception: 行く → 行って (ikte → itte)
す → して
話す → 話して hanasu → hanashite
貸す → 貸して kasu → kashite
つ/る/う → って
待つ → 待って matsu → matte
帰る → 帰って kaeru → kaette
買う → 買って kau → katte
む/ぶ/ぬ → んで
飲む → 飲んで nomu → nonde
遊ぶ → 遊んで asobu → asonde
死ぬ → 死んで shinu → shinde
Te-Form Power Patterns
〜ている / 〜てる — Ongoing / State
食べている = "is eating" / "eats (habitually)"
結婚している = "is married" (state)
〜てください — Please do
待ってください = "Please wait"
ゆっくり話してください = "Please speak slowly"
〜てもいい — It's OK to / May I?
食べてもいい？ = "Can I eat this?"
ここに座ってもいいですか？ = "May I sit here?"
〜てから — After doing
食べてから行く = "go after eating"
〜てみる — Try doing
食べてみて = "Try eating it"
やってみる = "I'll give it a try"
〜てしまう / 〜ちゃう — Ended up / regret
食べちゃった = "I ate it all (oops)"
忘れちゃった = "I totally forgot"

Adjectives

Japanese has two types: i-adjectives (end in い) and na-adjectives. They conjugate differently — and they conjugate differently from verbs too.

I-Adjectives — Conjugation
Plain present
おいしい oishii
Plain negative
おいしくない oishiku nai (drop い, add くない)
Plain past
おいしかった oishikatta (い → かった)
Past negative
おいしくなかった oishiku nakatta
Adverb form
おいしく oishiku (い → く)
Connecting (て)
おいしくて oishikute (い → くて)
Exception: いい (ii, "good") — it conjugates from its old form よい (yoi): よくない、よかった、よくて
Na-Adjectives — Conjugation
Plain present
好きだ suki da
Plain negative
好きじゃない suki ja nai
Plain past
好きだった suki datta
Past negative
好きじゃなかった suki ja nakatta
Modifying noun
好きな食べ物 suki na tabemono (add な before noun)
Connecting (で)
好きで suki de

Casual vs Formal Speech

Since you're learning everyday conversational Japanese, you'll mostly use casual. But knowing the difference — and when to switch — is key.

Casual (タメ語 tameguchi)

だ
copula (=is) for plain statements: 学生だ
〜ない
negative: 行かない (not going)
〜た
past: 食べた (ate)
〜てる
progressive: 食べてる (eating)
じゃない
negative copula: 学生じゃない
だった
past copula: 学生だった

Polite (丁寧語 teineigo)

です
polite copula: 学生です
〜ません
negative: 行きません
〜ました
past: 食べました
〜ています
progressive: 食べています
ではありません
negative copula (formal)
でした
past copula: 学生でした
Dropped Particles in Casual Speech

In real casual conversation, particles (especially は, を, が) are dropped all the time. This is completely normal.

TEXTBOOK / FORMAL
ご飯を食べましたか？
gohan wo tabemashita ka?
"Did you eat?"
REAL CASUAL
ご飯食べた？
gohan tabeta?
"Ate?" / "Did ya eat?"
TEXTBOOK / FORMAL
どこに行きますか？
doko ni ikimasu ka?
"Where are you going?"
REAL CASUAL
どこ行くの？
doko iku no?
"Where you going?"
Sentence-Ending Particles — The Flavor Words

These tiny words at the end of sentences add huge amounts of nuance — emotion, emphasis, seeking agreement, softness.

ね
Right? / Isn't it? — seeking agreement, softening
おいしいね — "This is good, right?"
よ
I'm telling you / you know — asserting information
それは違うよ — "That's wrong, I'm telling you"
よね
Right? (asserting but checking) — super common combo
そうだよね — "That's right, isn't it?"
の / の？
Soft question / explanation nuance (feminine-leaning)
どこ行くの？ — "Where are you going?"
かな
I wonder... / maybe... — talking to yourself / musing
大丈夫かな — "I wonder if it's okay..."
な
Talking to yourself / mild assertion (masculine)
疲れたな — "Man, I'm tired"
ぞ
Strong assertion (masculine, a bit rough)
行くぞ — "We're going!"
ぜ
Casual strong assertion (masculine, cool/friendly)
やるぜ — "Let's do it!"
っけ
Trying to remember / confirming something forgot
名前なんだっけ — "What was your name again?"
Real talk: ね、よ、and よね are used constantly in Japanese conversation. If you can sprinkle these in naturally, you'll sound dramatically more fluent. A flat statement without these sounds robotic.
When to Use Which?
Casual (タメ語)
Close friends, family, people your age or younger, people who invite casual speech, texting
Polite (丁寧語)
Strangers, shops/restaurants, work, teachers, elders, anyone you're meeting for the first time
Default as a learner
When in doubt, use polite. Japanese people won't be offended — they'll be impressed. Casual to the wrong person is rude; polite is just safe.
